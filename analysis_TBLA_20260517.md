**Inflection Signal:** ROE has been improving alongside CPO price recovery. The 2025 net income of IDR 900B represents strong absolute profitability for a company with IDR 4.05T market cap (P/E ~4.5×). The manufacturing segment (refinery, biodiesel, sugar factory) provides value-add margin on top of plantation revenue.

### Revenue Catalysts — Next 2 Quarters (Q2–Q3 2026)

1. **CPO Price Strength:** Palm oil futures at MYR 4,420/T (May 2026) are at cyclically elevated levels. Q2–Q3 is typically the peak production season, and higher CPO prices directly flow into plantation EBITDA margins. Every MYR 100/T CPO price increase adds ~IDR 80–120B to TBLA's annual net income.
2. **Biodiesel Mandate (B40 Implementation):** Indonesia's B40 biodiesel mandate (40% palm oil blended into diesel) creates captive domestic demand. TBLA's FAME (biodiesel) manufacturing capacity directly benefits from this program, insulating a portion of revenue from export price volatility.
3. **Sugar Season:** Indonesia's domestic sugar prices remain above import cost, benefiting TBLA's sugar factory operations. Sugar segment typically contributes 20–25% of total revenue.
4. **Cooking Oil Downstream Recovery:** Government stabilization of cooking oil prices in 2024–2025 has normalized the refined palm oil distribution environment. TBLA's branded cooking oil business should see volume recovery.

### Value Trap Check — Asset Turnover & Inventory

| Indicator | Reading | Interpretation |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| Asset Turnover | ~0.4–0.5× | Normal for plantation+manufacturing vertical |
| Inventory | Modest (perishable CPO — no inventory buildup risk) | Commodity processed quickly |
| Gross Margin | ~15–20% (manufacturing typical) | Competitive, not expanding |
| Net Margin (TTM) | 4.02% | Thin due to interest expense on IDR 10T+ debt |
| ROI | 8.81% | Improving — asset utilization increasing |

**Verdict:** The thin net margin (4.02%) is driven by **interest expense, not operational weakness**. Gross margins at the plantation level are 40–60%; the manufacturing segment compresses overall margins. This is a leverage story — if commodity prices remain elevated and debt is gradually reduced, ROE and net margins expand materially. NOT a structural value trap.

This is where TBLA becomes compelling:

- **Plantation Land Bank:** TBLA operates palm oil and sugarcane plantations across Sumatra and Kalimantan. A conservative 2× mark-to-market on plantation assets could add IDR 5–10T to NAV.
- **Sugar Factory:** Fully integrated sugar refinery — replacement value significantly exceeds book value.
- **Refinery Infrastructure:** Palm oil refinery, FAME (biodiesel) plant, and soap manufacturing facilities — all carried at depreciated book value.
- **Verdict:** Market cap of IDR 4.05T vs. NAV of ~IDR 17.78T = stock trades at **only 23 cents on the NAV dollar**. Even applying a 50% conglomerate discount, fair value should be IDR 8–9T market cap = IDR 1,350/share. The current price implies the market is pricing in near-bankruptcy risk — which is not supported by the financial data (IDR 900B net income in 2025).
