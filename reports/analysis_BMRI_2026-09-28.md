## Final Verdict: ⚡ Speculative Buy — MAINTAIN, ON WATCH

BMRI's two weeks since Sept 14 mark the most negative sustained stretch in this ticker's coverage. Price fell roughly 6% (from IDR 4,360 to ~IDR 4,100, with confirmed closes ranging 4,080-4,190) against a backdrop that combined a harsher-than-expected global risk-off (a *realized* Fed hike, +25bp to 3.75-4.00%, rather than the probabilistic risk flagged two weeks ago, plus rising oil prices and US Treasury yields that dragged IHSG down 4.35% for the week of Sept 21-25) with genuinely sustained, BMRI-specific foreign selling: BMRI was the single most-sold stock on the exchange in two consecutive weekly readings (Sept 14-18 and Sept 19-23) and again on Sept 24 alone, for a net outflow of roughly Rp514.5bn cumulative over the trailing month despite one bright day (Sept 17, +Rp208.4bn). Importantly, this selling pressure was shared with BBCA, BBRI, and BBNI in multiple reports — a sector-wide, flow-driven phenomenon consistent with a global hawkish repricing rather than a BMRI-specific credit deterioration, and no new NPL, margin, or asset-quality data has emerged to contradict the underlying fundamental thesis (Q3 earnings, the next hard data point, are still 4-5 weeks away). Bank Indonesia held its rate at 5.75% as expected in the first meeting under newly confirmed permanent Governor Destry Damayanti, and the Rp66/share interim dividend proceeded exactly on its confirmed schedule (payment due Oct 2, just days from this report) — genuine, if modest, positives. The critical new fact this window is mechanical rather than narrative: the sustained decline has pushed price to within roughly 1.2% of the unmoved IDR 4,050 hard stop, the tightest margin in this ticker's history in the series. This produces a nominal Reward/Risk figure (~30:1) that is actively misleading — the real message is that BMRI is now one bad session away from a stop-out, not that the trade has become dramatically safer. Valuation has become genuinely more attractive in isolation (P/E ~5.73x and P/B ~1.00x both now clearly below their -2SD bands, trailing yield ~11.6%), and the thesis is not broken, but the combination of a sustained three-week foreign-selling streak, a still-unresolved November MSCI decision, and a price sitting on top of its own stop-loss is a materially higher-risk setup than any prior cycle. The verdict remains Speculative Buy — the underlying loan-growth, capital-return, and re-rating thesis has not been invalidated by any new fundamental print — but the score is cut meaningfully to reflect the deteriorated momentum and macro-fit picture, and this position should be treated as on watch: a close below IDR 4,050 would call for reassessment rather than being explained away as noise a fourth time.
