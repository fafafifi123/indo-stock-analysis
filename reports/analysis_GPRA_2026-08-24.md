## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

### Value Trap Check

This remains the crux of the call, and the picture is now more nuanced than Aug 12 — genuinely better on the macro axis, unchanged-to-worse on the company-specific axis.

1. **The structural discount story is unchanged.** GPRA-type small-cap developers have traded at 0.25x–0.50x book for years without the gap closing.
2. **The sector-demand pillar of the bear case has genuinely weakened.** BI's Q2 2026 survey shows national home sales at -2.36% YoY, a dramatic swing from -25.67% YoY in Q1 — the single most bearish sector data point cited on Aug 12 has materially improved.
3. **But GPRA has not yet confirmed it is participating in that recovery.** Management's own H1 2026 language on marketing sales — "on track but not fully meeting expectations" — is verbatim unchanged from Aug 12, even as the sector-wide aggregate data improved sharply in the same window.
4. **Mortgage financing — the more GPRA-relevant channel — kept decelerating.** KPR growth slowed further to 4.4% YoY in June (from 5.1% in April), even as overall transaction *volume* recovered.
5. **The rights issue has become a larger, not smaller, wildcard.** Approved in principle at the June 29 RUPSLB with an "end of H1" disclosure target, it is now nearly two months past that self-set deadline with zero published terms.

**Conclusion: still a value trap read, but the gap between "sector recovering" and "GPRA confirmed to be recovering" is now the central open question.**

### Stress Tests

#### IDR Weakens to 19,500 — Still Negative, though the rupiah currently has more cushion
A ~10% rupiah depreciation from the current ~17,700 level would still compound imported-material cost pressure on gross margin, disproportionately affecting the Nemuru hotel's imported FF&E needs. The mechanism and direction are unchanged from Aug 12; the rupiah's modest recent strengthening provides a slightly larger buffer before this scenario bites.

#### BI Rate Path — Severe if a hike resumes, but the near-term hike risk has receded
BI held at 5.75% for a second straight meeting (Aug 18–19) — the first decision under Acting Governor Destry Damayanti following Perry Warjiyo's abrupt late-July departure — and explicitly signaled "room for further rate reductions" in 2026, a marked shift from the Aug-12 setup where a sell-side call flagged 6.25% as a live year-end risk. This meaningfully reduces the probability of the adverse +50bps scenario materializing near-term. However: (a) the new BI leadership is itself an unresolved uncertainty, and (b) even without further hikes, KPR mortgage growth kept decelerating (4.4% YoY June, down from 5.1% in April). A future BI cut, if delivered, would be a genuine positive catalyst for GPRA's mortgage-dependent buyer base — but as of Aug 24 it remains a signaled intention, not a delivered policy change.

#### MSCI Frontier Downgrade Nov 2026 — Minimal, already Avoid
Unchanged from Aug 12: GPRA's micro market cap (~USD 27–29mn) and thin free float mean it was never an MSCI Indonesia constituent, so a hypothetical downgrade would hit large-cap index names far more than GPRA directly.

## Final Verdict: 🔴 Avoid — MAINTAIN (score raised 23→25/70)

The thesis has genuinely moved since August 12, but not far enough, and not on the axis that matters most for a company-specific call. On the positive side, Bank Indonesia's Q2 2026 residential survey shows national home sales swinging from -25.67% YoY to -2.36% YoY — a dramatic softening of the sector-wide demand-destruction narrative that was the single most bearish data point in the prior report — alongside a firmer rupiah, a continuing IHSG rally, a new BI leadership team signaling room for rate cuts rather than further hikes, and an upward revision to GPRA's own Q2 profit. The Base-case DCF reference has, for the first time, moved modestly above the current price. On the negative side, none of this has yet shown up in GPRA's own numbers or in the stock: management's marketing-sales commentary is verbatim unchanged from Aug 12, the price has drifted down (112→109) rather than up, mortgage (KPR) credit growth continued decelerating even as overall sales volume recovered, and — most importantly — the ~Rp300bn rights issue approved at the June 29 RUPSLB is now nearly two months past management's own "end of H1" disclosure target with zero terms published, a slippage that reads as a fresh governance-opacity flag rather than a resolved risk. We raise the score modestly (23→25/70), landing exactly on the framework's Avoid/Hold boundary, and explicitly choose to hold the line at Avoid rather than round up, because the improvement is a sector aggregate GPRA has not yet confirmed at the company level, and the dilution overhang has grown rather than shrunk. This is the closest GPRA has been to a re-rating catalyst since coverage began. Legacy holders should continue managing risk with the stop-loss reference held at IDR 90 (unchanged); no fresh capital is warranted. Re-evaluate immediately upon rights-issue terms disclosure (now overdue) and at Q3 2026 results, specifically watching whether GPRA's own marketing sales/order book confirm the sector-wide demand recovery visible in the BI survey data.
