## Final Verdict: ⚡ Speculative Buy — MAINTAIN, Conviction Trimmed Further

This window brings the first genuine stall in the growth catalyst and the most advanced version yet of the CPO-cooling risk first flagged Sept 14 — but also the first resolved macro risk (a BI hold, not a hike) and a partial return to relative price outperformance versus the broad market. TBLA's price fell a further ~2.4% (625→610, confirmed Sept 25 close), extending its cumulative decline since Aug 12 to roughly −14.7% — but this occurred against a much sharper IHSG selloff (~−4.6% over the same fortnight, 6,541→6,241.89), driven by a realized Fed hike, rising oil, and rising US Treasury yields, meaning TBLA's decline was smaller than, not merely alongside, the broad-market move this time. The B50 catalyst, the central pillar of the thesis, held flat at ~94% station coverage rather than continuing to narrow — the first pause in this series' otherwise steady de-risking narrative — while CPO (Malaysia BMD) extended its pullback to RM4,673, a 7-week low now sitting at the top rather than the middle of the previously-flagged RM4,400–4,600 guidance band. Bank Indonesia's Sept 22–23 RDG resolved without a hawkish surprise (held at 5.75%, third consecutive hold, first meeting under permanent Governor Destry Damayanti), removing the single largest live risk flagged in the last two reports — even as the rupiah itself genuinely weakened for the first time in this series, a mild tailwind for TBLA's USD-linked revenue but also a signal of the same capital-outflow stress hitting the broader index. For the first time, TBLA-specific technical and foreign-flow data (single-sourced, uncorroborated) confirmed a bearish setup: a death cross, RSI near oversold at 30, and average daily net foreign selling of ~2,979 lots. We maintain Speculative Buy, hold both the base target (IDR 1,100) and hard stop (IDR 580) flat, and note that the Bear-case DCF value now sits exactly at the confirmed spot price (IDR 610) — on the model's own terms, the downside scenario has already arrived, leaving the stop at 580 (−4.9%, the tightest cushion in this series) as the effective remaining tail-risk boundary. The R/R improvement to 16.33:1 is mechanical, not fundamental. Score moves down from 50 to 46, reflecting a window where the growth catalyst paused and the commodity backdrop deteriorated further, only partially offset by a resolved rate-decision risk and relative price resilience — this remains a high-conviction, high-volatility satellite position, but the balance of new information this window is, on net, more negative than any prior checkpoint in the series.
