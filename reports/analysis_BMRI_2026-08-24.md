### Stress Tests

#### IDR Weakens to 19,500 (~10% depreciation from the new ~17,700–17,720 base, vs ~9% at Aug 12's ~17,820)
Unchanged mechanism from Aug 12, restated against the now-stronger starting point: BI would likely resume hiking to defend the currency (note HSBC's Aug 20 flag that further hikes "can't be ruled out" given El Niño inflation risk — a real, if not-yet-triggered, path to this scenario), pressuring loan growth and adding to NIM headwinds. Foreign outflows would likely accelerate beyond the already-observed Aug 13–14 MSCI-linked spike. BMRI's AFS/bond book would take mark-to-market losses as yields rise; direct FX P&L exposure remains limited (broadly currency-matched book). Expected outcome: retest of the 3,950 stop/support zone, which — given the rupiah's actual trajectory this window has been the opposite (strengthening, not weakening) — looks like a lower-probability near-term scenario than it did on Aug 12, though it remains the correct tail case to model.

#### BI Rate +50bps (to 6.25%)
Mechanism unchanged from Aug 12 and now slightly more topical: the Aug 18–19 hold was BI's second straight, but it was also the first decision under Acting Governor Destry Damayanti, and HSBC explicitly flagged (Aug 20) that El Niño-driven food-price risk keeps a hike on the table before year-end. Net effect for BMRI would likely remain **mildly positive** near-term (CASA-heavy funding, ~65% CASA, reprices slower than loan yields, so a hike widens spreads before it compresses volume growth), with the credit-side offset (SME/consumer debt-service stress, provisioning lag 1–2 quarters) unchanged from the prior assessment.

#### MSCI Frontier Downgrade — November 2026 (now informed by a completed Aug 13 "dry run")
This remains the largest single risk to the thesis, and it is no longer purely hypothetical: the **August 13, 2026 quarterly review already happened**, cutting Indonesia's MSCI free-float market cap from ~US$80bn to ~US$74bn (CPIN downgraded to Small Cap, GOTO removed from Global Standard) and triggering an estimated ~US$753mn passive outflow — of which BMRI's ~IDR 815bn single-week net sell (week of Aug 14) was a visible, concentrated slice. Crucially, that selling pressure **crested and faded within about a week** (down to ~IDR 40bn net sell by Aug 19), and the stock recovered to be the top Big-4 gainer by Aug 21 without threatening the 3,950/3,900 support zone. This is the clearest real-world evidence yet for the thesis's core claim — that MSCI-driven flow events get absorbed by domestic buyers rather than triggering a structural breakdown. The **binding November 2026 review remains unresolved**: reforms (free float raised to 15%, HSC disclosure framework) are characterized by MSCI as "a step in the right direction," but "consistent implementation" is still being assessed, and a Frontier-track consultation is not off the table. If November delivers a larger, structural version of what August delivered in miniature, the expected outcome is unchanged from Aug 12: a flush toward the 3,650–3,900 zone, which the thesis assumes would again be absorbed by domestic buyers at deeply discounted multiples (P/E <6x, yield >11%). This remains the single event most likely to invalidate the stop.

## Final Verdict: ⚡ Speculative Buy — MAINTAIN

Twelve days on from the Aug 12 report, BMRI's core thesis is unchanged and, on balance, marginally strengthened rather than tested. Price is essentially flat (IDR 4,220 → ~4,230, +0.2%) and no new fundamental data has printed — Q3 2026 earnings remain the next hard catalyst, due late October — so the "valuation gap" identified on Aug 12 (P/E 5.9x, P/B ~1.0x, dividend yield ~11.3%, all screening below/above their 5-year -2SD/+2SD bands against an 18.6% annualized ROE and 19.9% loan growth) has neither closed via re-rating nor reversed via deterioration; it has simply persisted through a genuinely eventful macro window. That window delivered three real developments — a surprise Bank Indonesia governor transition (Perry Warjiyo's July 25 resignation, Destry Damayanti's elevation) that was tested and passed cleanly at the Aug 18–19 rate decision (held at 5.75%, second straight hold); a completed MSCI Index Review on Aug 13 that cut Indonesia's passive weight and triggered a concentrated, visible wave of foreign selling in BMRI (~IDR 815bn in a single week) that then faded within days without breaking support; and a rupiah that kept strengthening (~17,820 → ~17,700–17,720) rather than the hawkish-tail scenario this report's stress tests continue to flag. The Aug 13 event functions as a genuine, if partial, dry run for the binding November 2026 MSCI decision, and the market's response to it — sharp but short-lived selling, absorbed by domestic buyers, no stop-zone test — is exactly the pattern this thesis has argued for since the June 29 spring low. That gives grounds for a modest score increase (52→54/70, driven by improved Momentum and Macro Fit) and a tightened stop (3,900→3,950, reflecting eleven weeks of untested support), which together lift the base-case reward/risk to roughly 4.9:1 from 4.3:1 — even as targets, price, and the fundamental case itself are carried forward unchanged. This remains a *speculative*, not outright, Buy because November's MSCI outcome is still genuinely binary and macro-driven, and August's dry run — however encouraging — is not proof of how a full Frontier-track consultation would trade; but each week the spring low goes untested and each flow event gets absorbed without a fundamental crack appearing adds incremental confidence that any MSCI-driven flush would again be a buying opportunity rather than a fundamental impairment.
