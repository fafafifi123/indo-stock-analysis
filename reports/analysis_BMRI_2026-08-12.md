## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

### Value Trap Check (loan book quality)

The multiple compression here is **not** being driven by credit deterioration — that is the key distinction from a genuine value trap:
- NPL ratio remains low by regional standards (historically ~1.0–1.2% consolidated) with loan loss coverage comfortably above 280%.
- COVID-era restructured/special-mention loans have largely worked off the book by 2026.
- The fast-growing government/SOE ecosystem book (+41.6% YoY, now IDR 489tn) is quasi-sovereign risk — low probability of default, but it does compress yields and concentrates exposure to public-sector capex cycles. This is the one area worth monitoring for pricing discipline, not asset quality.
- Bottom line: EPS and book value are still compounding (ROE ~18.6% annualized, H1 profit +24.4% YoY). The stock is cheap because of index/flow dynamics (MSCI overhang, foreign selling), not because the franchise is impaired. That is a reversal setup, not a trap.

### Stress Tests

#### IDR Weakens to 19,500 (~9% depreciation from ~17,820)
BI would likely resume hiking to defend the currency, pressuring loan growth and adding NIM headwinds on top of the existing compression trend. Foreign outflows would probably accelerate. BMRI has limited direct FX P&L exposure (loans/deposits broadly currency-matched), but its bond/AFS portfolio would take mark-to-market losses as yields rise. Expected outcome: retest of the 3,900 stop/support zone; the position would need to be reassessed if the stop is breached on a closing basis.

#### BI Rate +50bps (to 6.25%)
Net effect is likely **mildly positive** for near-term earnings: BMRI's CASA-heavy funding base (~65% CASA) reprices slower than loan yields, so a hike widens spreads before it compresses volume growth. Risk is on the credit side — SME and consumer segments would see debt-service stress rise first, potentially lifting provisioning with a lag of 1–2 quarters. Loan growth guidance (currently tracking 19.9% YoY vs. ~16–18% full-year guidance) would likely decelerate toward the low end.

#### MSCI Frontier Downgrade — November 2026
This remains the largest single risk to the thesis and is now on a **confirmed calendar date** rather than a vague overhang: MSCI has extended Indonesia's review to November 2026, contingent on free-float (targeting 15% minimum) and transparency reforms (OJK/IDX/KSEI disclosure changes, the new High Shareholding Concentration framework) showing "adequate progress." A downgrade consultation would trigger passive-fund outflows disproportionately concentrated in BMRI as IDX's most liquid EM-index constituent — plausibly IDR 3–6tn (~USD 170–340mn) of passive selling pressure, on top of the active foreign selling already visible today. Expected outcome: a flush back toward the 3,650–3,900 zone, similar in shape to the June 29 spring, which the current thesis assumes would again be absorbed by domestic buyers at deeply discounted multiples (P/E <6x, yield >11%). This is the single event most likely to invalidate the stop.

## Final Verdict: 🟡 Speculative Buy — MAINTAIN

Since the July 4 report, BMRI's operating performance has materially outrun its share price: H1 2026 consolidated net profit grew 24.4% YoY on loan growth nearly double the industry average, funding costs improved, and ROE is inflecting higher even as NIM compresses — yet the stock is essentially flat at IDR 4,220, pushing the trailing P/E to 5.9x (below the 5Y -2SD band) and the trailing dividend yield to 11.3% (forward ~12.8–13.3%). That widening gap between fundamentals and price is the core of this report's upgrade in *conviction* (score 49→52/70) even as the letter verdict is unchanged. The gap exists because the tape is currently a flow story, not a credit story: persistent — if inconsistent — foreign net selling, culminating in BMRI topping today's foreign net-sell list despite a broad +1.69% MSCI-day rally in IHSG, and a now firmly dated November 2026 MSCI Frontier-reclassification decision that remains the dominant calendar risk. The June 29 spring low at IDR 3,940 has held twice, support the case for a tightened stop at 3,900 and an add zone of 3,950–4,150, materially improving the reward/risk profile to roughly 4.3:1 on the base case. This remains a *speculative* rather than outright Buy because the MSCI outcome is genuinely binary and macro-driven rather than fundamentals-driven — but the earnings trajectory gives more, not less, confidence that any MSCI-driven flush would again be a buying opportunity rather than a fundamental impairment.
