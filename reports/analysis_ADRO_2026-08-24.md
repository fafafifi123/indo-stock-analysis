### Stress Tests

#### IDR Weakens to 19,500 — POSITIVE
Unchanged mechanism from Aug 12: ADRO/Alamtri's metcoal and mining-services revenue is USD-linked while a meaningful share of operating costs are IDR-denominated, so rupiah weakness (from ~17,650–17,750 to 19,500, roughly -10%) would expand IDR-reported margins and dividend capacity. This dynamic is unaffected by the BI leadership transition — if anything, the governor-resignation episode (which briefly weakened the IDR before BI's steady-hand August hold restored calm) was a live, small-scale demonstration of ADRO's role as a natural rupiah hedge within the portfolio.

#### BI Rate Path — Neutral, with a new watch item
D/E of ~0.12x (assumed unchanged) means ADRO carries minimal rate-sensitive IDR debt and a largely USD revenue base, so a further hike would primarily pressure domestic-demand sectors rather than ADRO's P&L directly — the Aug 12 read holds. The new variable is the BI leadership transition itself: Acting Governor Destry Damayanti's first meeting (Aug 18–19) held rates at 5.75% and signaled continuity, but a permanent governor has not yet been confirmed, and the manner of Perry Warjiyo's exit (reported policy rift with the Finance Ministry over liquidity policy) introduces a policy-predictability risk that didn't exist on Aug 12. For ADRO specifically the direct impact is still assessed as low given the balance sheet, but this is now a macro variable worth monitoring for its indirect effect on the IDR and on foreign risk appetite for Indonesian equities broadly.

#### MSCI Frontier Downgrade — November 2026 — MODERATE NEGATIVE (unchanged)
No material change since Aug 12: Indonesia's review remains extended to November 2026, with reforms (HSC shareholder-disclosure framework, a roadmap toward a 15% minimum free float) underway at OJK/IDX/KSEI but assessed by MSCI as insufficient as of its June review (outlook cut to negative). Estimated passive-outflow risk if downgraded: $2.2–13B market-wide. As a large, liquid, dividend-paying constituent, ADRO would likely see proportional index-fund selling in a downgrade scenario, partially offset by domestic institutional demand and the dividend yield — impact remains assessed as moderate, and this stays the key swing risk into year-end.

## Final Verdict: 💰 Hold (Income) — MAINTAIN, R/R IMPROVED

The twelve days since Aug 12 delivered an unusually clean version of the setup this framework looks for: the stock barely moved (+2.4% to IDR 2,580) while the underlying story visibly firmed. KAI's aluminum smelter converted "on track" language into realized export shipments to the US and South Korea, full-capacity guidance was pulled forward to September–October, Newcastle coal held a steady $130–133/t floor, and three separate sell-side desks reaffirmed or raised targets into the IDR 3,200–3,300 range. The result is that risk/reward actually improved, from 1.43:1 to 1.58:1, even with the stock sitting close to its 52-week high — the opposite of the July→August pattern where price outran fundamentals. Two genuine new uncertainties are worth flagging rather than a reason to change the call: (1) no H1/Q2 2026 earnings print has surfaced yet in available sources, so the "genuine or partial ASP recovery" question is still resting on the 1Q26 print plus guidance, not a second confirming quarter; and (2) Bank Indonesia's governor resigned abruptly in late July amid reported friction with the Finance Ministry, and while the August rate decision and the acting governor's "continuity" signaling calmed markets, a permanent appointment is still pending. Neither changes the core thesis — dividend yield (~10.6–10.7%), balance sheet strength (D/E ~0.12x, assumed unchanged), and the structural de-risking from the AADI demerger remain supportive — and the base case still implies ~20% upside to IDR 3,100. But the stock is technically overbought into a resistance test (2,580–2,590), so this is not yet a breakout to chase either. Maintain Hold, harvest the dividend, trail the stop to IDR 2,250, and reserve fresh buying for a pullback into the IDR 2,350–2,450 add zone or a confirmed break above the IDR 2,590–2,700 pivot zone. Watch the H1/Q2 earnings release and the November MSCI review as the two catalysts most likely to move this from Hold to an active Buy or back toward a defensive stance.
