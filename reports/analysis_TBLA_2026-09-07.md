### Growth Catalyst (Q3–Q4 2026)

The central catalyst has **strengthened materially and de-risked further** since Aug 24:
- **B50 station coverage jumped from ~57% (Aug 24) to ~80% nationwide** by late August/early September, per Antara News infographic reporting, with Pertamina's own retail network — the dominant channel — already at roughly **90%** as of Aug 31. This is a large, concrete jump in the two-week window and substantially narrows the remaining gap to the October 1, 2026 full-compliance deadline.
- The government has also formalized a **transition mechanism**: SPBUs have until **September 30, 2026** to exhaust remaining B40 stock before mandatory full B50 distribution begins Oct 1 — an orderly wind-down structure that reduces the risk of a chaotic last-minute scramble.
- **Residual gap, now narrow and geographically concentrated:** Papua and Maluku — roughly 399 SPBUs and 21 fuel terminals — had reportedly not yet begun B50 distribution as of the most recent reporting. This is a small (single-digit percentage) share of the national station count, but a genuine, named execution risk rather than a diffuse "43% somewhere" gap as characterized on Aug 24. Watch for whether this pocket closes by Oct 1 or requires a formal regional carve-out/delay.
- TBLA's **722,000 KL 2026 biodiesel delivery contract** is reconfirmed unchanged in this window (referenced again in early-September sector commentary); no evidence of an allocation revision.
- **CPO prices held near/above the Aug 20–21 20-month high** (RM4,950–5,018/tonne through early September) rather than reverting — reinforcing rather than fading the upstream-margin tailwind flagged Aug 24, with one report explicitly noting Indonesia's decision to *maintain* B50 (rather than escalate to B60) as a limiting-but-not-reversing factor on further CPO upside.

Net: the single biggest swing factor in this report is the **B50 coverage jump from ~57% to ~80%** — a substantially de-risked path to the Oct 1 deadline, with the remaining gap now concentrated in a small, named, low-share region rather than a broad national shortfall.

Two weeks after the Aug 24 report flagged a fundamentals-outrunning-price divergence, that divergence has **widened, not closed**. CPO prices held near their highest levels in over a year rather than reverting, the IHSG kept making fresh highs, and the rupiah kept strengthening modestly — every macro and sector tailwind flagged on Aug 24 either held or extended. Yet TBLA's own share price fell a further ~3.0% (660→640, now confirmed by two independent sources rather than estimated), taking the cumulative move since Aug 12 to roughly −10.5% against an IHSG that is up ~4.5% over the same span — and this occurred in a tape where foreign investors were net buyers of the broader market, arguing for an idiosyncratic rather than macro-driven explanation.
