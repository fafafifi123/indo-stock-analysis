### Stress Tests

#### IDR Weakens to 19,000 (~7–8% depreciation from the current ~17,630–17,760 base)
This window provided a live, if partial, test of this scenario: the US–Iran conflict's oil-price shock pushed IDR to ~17,744 (Sep 1) before it recovered to ~17,630 by Sep 4 — a real-world instance of exactly the kind of external shock this stress test contemplates, and the rupiah proved more resilient than the headline risk implied. A fuller ~19,000 scenario (vs. the ~19,500 case modeled on Aug 24, rebased to the current, slightly stronger starting point) would still likely see BI lean toward defending the currency — though it now cannot do so at its next scheduled opportunity until the Sep 22–23 RDG, meaning any acute FX stress before then would have to be absorbed without a fresh rate-decision lever. Mechanism otherwise unchanged from Aug 24: pressured loan growth, added NIM headwind, likely accelerated foreign outflows, AFS/bond mark-to-market losses, limited direct FX P&L exposure (broadly currency-matched book). Given the actual rupiah path this window (a shock and a partial recovery, not a sustained slide), this remains a lower-probability near-term scenario, but the live US–Iran tail risk keeps it more topical than a purely theoretical case.

#### BI Rate Decision — September 22–23, 2026 (unresolved, falls after this report)
This is now a **live, dated, near-term event** rather than a background assumption: BI has not met since Aug 18–19 and will not meet again until Sep 22–23, after this report's publication date. No market-implied probability data for that meeting was found in this window's research — flagged as a genuine information gap, not an omission. Mechanism carried forward from Aug 24: a hold (base case, consistent with two prior holds under Acting Governor Destry Damayanti) would be broadly neutral; a hike (e.g., in response to the US–Iran-driven oil/FX shock, or El Niño food-price risk previously flagged by HSBC) would likely remain mildly positive for BMRI near-term given its CASA-heavy funding (loans reprice faster than deposits), with credit-side offsets (SME/consumer debt-service stress) lagging 1–2 quarters; a cut would support loan growth and volumes but tighten NIM. This meeting is the single most concrete scheduled catalyst before the next report.

#### MSCI Frontier Downgrade — November 2026 (no new decision this window; evidence continues to accumulate in the thesis's favor)
Unchanged in substance from Aug 24: the August 13 review remains the only completed data point, and the binding November 2026 review — with its associated free-float/ownership-disclosure reform checklist — is still unresolved. What is new this window is not the MSCI decision itself but a second consecutive round of evidence on how the market handles MSCI-adjacent flow shocks: BMRI's foreign-selling episode did not just fade, it **fully reversed into three weeks of sustained net buying** (see Section 4). If November delivers a larger, structural version of the August rebalance, the expected outcome per the existing framework is unchanged — a flush toward the 3,650–3,900 zone that the thesis assumes domestic buyers would again absorb at deeply discounted multiples. The accumulating pattern of absorption-then-reversal across two consecutive windows modestly strengthens confidence in that assumption, without in any way resolving the underlying binary risk. This remains the single event most likely to invalidate the (now trailed-up) stop.

## Final Verdict: ⚡ Speculative Buy — MAINTAIN

Two weeks on from Aug 24, BMRI's core thesis is unchanged, and the price action has moved firmly in the thesis's favor even as that same move has mechanically eaten into the valuation and reward/risk edge that made the trade attractive in the first place. The headline development is a clean reversal of the mid-August MSCI-linked foreign-selling wave: after a ~IDR 815bn single-week net sell around the Aug 13 rebalance and a subsequent fade, foreign investors turned to sustained, sizeable net buying in BMRI across the last week of August and first week of September (~IDR 582bn, then accelerating to several-hundred-billion-rupiah single sessions), pushing the stock from IDR 4,230 to IDR 4,420 (+4.5%) as IHSG itself climbed roughly 2% to the mid-6,600s. Layered on top of that flow story is a genuinely new fundamental-adjacent data point — a Rp66/share FY2026 interim dividend (~IDR 6.16tn total) declared Sep 3 — that confirms management is proceeding with its capital-return program on schedule. Working against the thesis, purely mechanically: the price rally has narrowed the valuation gap (P/E moving from clearly below its -2SD band toward sitting almost exactly on it; P/B similarly less extreme; trailing yield compressing from ~11.3% to ~10.8%) and compressed the base-case reward/risk from 4.9:1 to 3.2:1, even after trailing the stop up from 3,950 to 4,050 on the back of thirteen weeks of untested spring-low support. Macro conditions were more eventful than fundamental: an escalating US–Iran conflict pushed oil toward ~US$91/bbl and briefly weakened the rupiah before a partial recovery — a live instance of exactly the kind of external shock this report's stress tests have long flagged, which the currency absorbed without lasting damage — while Bank Indonesia has not met since Aug 18–19 and won't again until Sep 22–23, leaving the 5.75% rate and the Acting Governor framing entirely unchanged for now but setting up the next RDG as the most concrete near-term catalyst to watch. MSCI's binding November 2026 decision remains completely unresolved, with no new information this window beyond incremental detail on the reform checklist (the 15% minimum free-float roadmap, ownership-disclosure thresholds) — but the second consecutive instance of a foreign-selling shock fully reversing (first the Aug 13–21 fade, now a full flip to net buying) incrementally strengthens, without resolving, the report's central bet that MSCI-driven flow events in this name are absorbed rather than compounding into a structural breakdown. Net effect: this remains a *speculative*, not outright, Buy, with the score ticking down marginally (54→53/70) to reflect the narrower valuation cushion and reward/risk that a real, if welcome, price advance necessarily produces — the risk profile has improved qualitatively (support tested and held, flows reversed, a new dividend declared) even as it has become numerically tighter.
