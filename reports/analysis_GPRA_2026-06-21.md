## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

### Is This a Value Trap? — YES

**Classic Value Trap Conditions Present:**

1. **Cheap on P/B (apparent):** Property companies carry land at historical cost. But land value is irrelevant if you cannot SELL the units built on it at profitable prices.

2. **Revenue recognition illusion:** The +36.85% QoQ revenue jump reflects projects completed and handed over that were SOLD 12–24 months ago at pre-rate-hike prices. Current demand pipeline is weak.

3. **Inventory risk:** Unsold completed units generate carrying costs with no offsetting revenue. As rates stay elevated, this inventory depreciates in real terms and requires markdown to clear.

4. **Forward P/E of 18–21x:** On current earnings run rate, GPRA is EXPENSIVE, not cheap. For a property company in a rate-hiking cycle, this multiple is indefensible.

5. **Cash trap:** Development WIP ties up large amounts of cash that cannot be quickly liquidated. In a rate-hiking environment, this cash position is a liability.

### Hidden Assets Check

| Asset | Notes | Conclusion |
|-------|-------|------------|
| Land Bank (Greater Jakarta suburbs) | Valued at cost; unrealized gains possible | Illiquid; declining in real terms |
| Completed unsold inventory | Burden at 12%+ KPR rates | Needs markdown to sell |
| Presales pipeline | Contracted but unrecognized revenue | Slowing take-up rates |

**Land Bank Reality:** GPRA's suburban Greater Jakarta land bank does have intrinsic value, but: (1) suburban property prices have weakened in this rate cycle, (2) liquidity of land assets is near-zero in distress, (3) development capital costs 12%+ to borrow. The hidden asset is real but cannot be monetized in the current environment.
